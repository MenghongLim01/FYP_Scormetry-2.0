                                           ABSTRACT


       This manuscript proposes the development of Scormetry 2.0: AI Rubric Generation and

Secure Document Management for Academic Evaluations to improve rubric-based defenses and

academic presentations at Paragon International University. In current practice, student reports

and slides are often shared through multiple channels such as Google Classroom, email,

messaging apps, or student devices. This leads to common issues during defense sessions,

including missing files, difficulty locating documents quickly, and opening the wrong version

under time-limited conditions.


       The project introduces a centralized, web-based platform organized through

controlled-access team rooms. Students upload the final report and slides into one secure

location, while judges can access only the rooms assigned to them. Scormetry 2.0 also

implements a dynamic scoring workflow based on the official fixed rubric, where

criterion-by-criterion scoring and automatic calculation reduce manual setup time and minimize

formula or weighting errors commonly seen in spreadsheet-based scoring.


       To    preserve   standardization,   Scormetry   2.0   includes   an   AI-assisted   rubric

PDF-to-dynamic conversion process with organizer verification and rubric locking before

scoring begins. Scores and feedback (comments, notes, and follow-up points) are stored together

in one centralized record for post-defense review. The system is developed iteratively and

evaluated through user acceptance testing aligned with selected ISO/IEC 25010 quality

characteristics to assess usability, security, reliability, and overall fitness for ParagonIU

evaluation tasks.

                           8.1 Rationale / Background of the Study


       Final Year Project (FYP) defenses and academic presentations at Paragon International

University involve multiple teams, judges, and strict time constraints. In practice, student

documents (reports and slides) are often scattered across messaging apps and shared drives,

leading to missing files, wrong versions, and delays during defense sessions. Additionally, rubric

setup and score consolidation are frequently done manually, which is time-consuming and prone

to calculation errors—especially when there are many criteria and judges. These challenges

reduce evaluation efficiency, increase stress during the event, and make post-defense review

difficult. Scormetry 2.0 was developed to address these issues by providing controlled access to

documents, verified dynamic rubric conversion, and centralized scoring and feedback in one

platform.

system that supports secure document access, validated dynamic rubric conversion, and

centralized evaluation outputs for academic defenses. Specifically, it answered the following

       files, and difficulty finding files quickly?


   3.​ How can the official (fixed) rubric be implemented dynamically to reduce manual setup

       calculation?


   4.​ How can the system perform verified and locked dynamic rubric conversion from the

       review anytime after the defense?

                                             8.3 Findings


       Based on the results of system development, functional testing, and evaluation using

hypothetical event scenarios, the following findings were obtained:


   1.​ A role-based and assignment-based access control approach (admin/organizer, judge,

       team member) effectively restricted access so judges could only view assigned team

       rooms, while organizers could monitor the overall event status.

   2.​ A centralized team-room document workflow with upload validation, clear naming rules,

       and version indicators improved document availability and reduced confusion caused by

       wrong or outdated files.

   3.​ A dynamic rubric module with pre-defined criteria, weights, and automated calculations

       reduced scoring errors and eliminated the need for manual formula setup during events.

   4.​ The dynamic rubric conversion workflow supported verified extraction and locking of

       rubric structures from the official rubric source, enabling consistent scoring, centralized

       judge feedback, and consolidated results for post-defense review.

                                           8.4 Conclusions


Based on the findings, the conclusions below are hereby presented:


   1.​ A controlled-access model based on user roles and team/judge assignments is suitable for

       ensuring confidentiality and proper access to evaluation documents and records.

   2.​ Centralizing documents in team rooms with version control indicators supports quick

       retrieval and improves readiness during time-limited defense sessions.

   3.​ Transformation of the official rubric into a dynamic rubric with automated calculations

       improves accuracy and reduces manual workload, especially in multi-judge evaluations.

   4.​ Verified and locked dynamic rubric conversion enables consistent evaluation standards

       and supports effective consolidation of scores and feedback for post-defense reporting.

                                     8.5 Recommendations


Based on the conclusions, the following recommendations are proposed:


   1.​ Improve defense readiness and document compliance. Implement a defense readiness

       checklist (required files + latest version confirmation) and scheduled reminders so teams

       upload final documents before the deadline and judges can review in advance.

   2.​ Enhance rubric governance. Expand the rubric module to support rubric variations per

       department/program, but keep an approval workflow (verify → lock) to prevent

       unverified rubric changes during scoring.

   3.​ Add reporting and analytics features. Provide export options (PDF/Excel) for scores and

       feedback, plus dashboards (submission status, scoring distribution, judge participation) to

       support post-defense review and academic decision-making.

   4.​ Integrate QR code-based voting and centralized event feedback. A future version of the

       system may include QR code access for voting, judging, or participant feedback so users

       can quickly submit evaluations through their own devices. This can make the system

       more convenient for large-scale events and allow scores, votes, and comments to be

       stored in one centralized place for later review, reporting, and decision-making.

                                           INTRODUCTION


       This chapter presents the background and context of the study, focusing on current

evaluation practices used in Final Year Project defenses and academic events at Paragon

International University. It discusses the key challenges faced by judges and organizers in

document handling and scoring workflows, which may affect efficiency and accuracy during

evaluations. The chapter then introduces Scormetry 2.0 as a proposed solution to improve secure

document management and dynamic scoring using the official rubric. Finally, it outlines the

problem statement, objectives, and the scope of the project to guide the overall development and

research direction.


1.1 CONTEXT OF THE STUDY


       Paragon International University conducts many rubric-based academic evaluations,

including Final Year Project (FYP) defenses, class presentations, and other assessments

involving multiple judges and evaluation teams. In practice, these evaluations often rely on

separate tools for document sharing, scoring, and feedback, resulting in a fragmented workflow

that can be time-consuming, inconsistent, and difficult to manage efficiently. Brookhart (2018)

explained that “True rubrics feature criteria appropriate to an assessment’s purpose”; therefore,

the fairness and consistency of rubric-based evaluation depend not only on the rubric itself but

also on the ability of evaluators to access and apply it properly in real assessment settings. Red

Deer Polytechnic (2023) further noted that “Rubrics can reduce grading and feedback time and

ensure shared expectations”. Nevertheless, delays may still occur when final student documents,
                                                                                                 2

scoring instruments, and feedback records are not centralized, especially during time-sensitive

defense sessions.


       The original Scormetry (2025) project provided the initial foundation for addressing this

issue by focusing on digital score tabulation for rubric-based evaluations at Paragon International

University. The system helped reduce manual scoring work and improved the organization of

evaluation results. Based on this existing foundation, Scormetry 2.0 is not a completely new

system, but rather an implementation and enhancement of the Scormetry base. It extends the

previous system by adding secure document upload and access through controlled team rooms,

AI-assisted transformation of the official rubric PDF into a dynamic rubric for scoring without

changing its approved structure, and centralized storage of scores and judge feedback for

post-evaluation review. In this way, Scormetry 2.0 strengthens and expands the original

Scormetry system into a more integrated platform for managing rubric-based academic

evaluations.

1.3 PROJECT DESCRIPTION


       Scormetry 2.0 is a web-based evaluation support system for Paragon International

University designed for rubric-based academic evaluations such as Final Year Project (FYP)

defenses and similar presentations. The system organizes each evaluation within a secure,

team-based room that supports controlled access for assigned judges and other authorized users.

Students upload final documents, including the report and presentation slides, into their team

room for access before and during the defense.


       Scormetry 2.0 includes an AI-assisted rubric PDF to dynamic rubric conversion

workflow that transforms the official department rubric into a structured rubric format, followed

by verification and locking to preserve the approved rubric structure. Judges assign scores for

each criterion using the dynamic rubric, and the system automatically calculates totals and

tabulates the results. Feedback, including comments, notes, and follow-up points, is stored

together with scoring records and documents inside the team room for later review.


       Overall, Scormetry 2.0 aims to improve evaluation readiness, scoring consistency, and

the secure organization of evaluation records by centralizing documents, scoring, and feedback

within one controlled-access platform.

1.10 DEFINITIONS OF TERMS


       This section presents the operational and technical terms used in this study to ensure clear

understanding of Scormetry 2.0 concepts and system features.


Operational Terms


   ●​ Team Room: A controlled-access space assigned to each student team where final

       documents, scoring records, and feedback are stored in one place.


   ●​ Assigned Judge: A judge or instructor associated with a specific team room and

       authorized to access documents and submit evaluation scores.


   ●​ Document Management: Uploading, storing, organizing, and retrieving student reports

       and slides in one centralized location.
                                                                                         15


   ●​ Feedback: Comments, notes, suggestions, and follow-up questions recorded by judges

      and saved with the scoring record.


   ●​ Scoring and Tabulation: Entering scores per criterion and automatically calculating

      totals/averages to produce final results.


Technical Terms


   ●​ Access Control: Security rules that restrict who can view, upload, or manage documents

      and evaluation records.


   ●​ Dynamic Rubric: A structured system-based version of the official rubric used for

      criterion-based scoring, automatic calculation, and final tabulation.

                  REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES


        This chapter reviews related literature and studies aligned with the Statement of the

Problem (SOPs). It presents evidence related to (1) centralized student document access for

evaluation, (2) access control models for protecting evaluation materials, (3) Dynamic rubric

implementation and automated scoring to reduce manual workload and errors, and (4)

centralized storage of scores and feedback for post-defense review.


2.1 Related Literature: Centralized Document Access and Management


        Ndibalema (2022) reports that the transition to online distance learning in

developing-country HEIs was “challenged with unstable network connection” and unaffordable

bandwidth, and emphasizes that effective implementation requires stable and affordable internet

accessibility.


        Currier et al. (2004) emphasize that “Metadata enables users to find the resources they

require.” In defense settings, consistent labeling and structured organization (e.g., team,

document type, version, and upload date) support faster retrieval and reduce the risk of opening

an incorrect version.


2.2 Related Literature: Access Control Models (RBAC/ABAC) for Secure Evaluation


        Sandhu et al. (1996) explain that “permissions are associated with roles, and users are

assigned to appropriate roles.” This describes role-based access control (RBAC), where access is

managed through defined roles such as Judge, Student, and Organizer/Admin.
                                                                                                17


       Hu et al. (2014) define ABAC as “a logical access control methodology where

authorization to perform a set of operations is determined by evaluating attributes associated

with the subject, object, requested operations, and, in some cases, environment conditions

against policy.” This enables more specific access decisions using attributes such as team ID,

judge assignment, defense round, and document type, which is relevant for restricting access to

team rooms and evaluation records.


2.3 Related Literature: Dynamic Rubrics and Automated Scoring Accuracy


       Brookhart and Chen (2014) emphasize that “rubrics yield information of sufficient

quality if certain conditions are met, most notably having clear and focused criteria.” This

highlights the importance of implementing the official rubric in a clear and consistent dynamic

format to improve scoring consistency across judges.


       Maditinos et al. (2012) warn that “errors contained in most spreadsheets may be

catastrophic and difficult to detect.” This highlights the risk of manual spreadsheet-based scoring

and supports automated calculation and standardized dynamic scoring to reduce formula and

weighting errors.


2.4 Related Literature: Feedback and Record Management in Assessments


       JISC (2010) explains that technology can add value to assessment activities, including

“the processes involved in submission, marking and return of assessed assignments” and “the

generation of feedback.” This shows the importance of organizing assessment outputs so that

marks and feedback can be recorded, stored, and retrieved through one structured process for

post-evaluation review.​
                                                                                                18


​      McTighe et al. (2022) explain that analytic rubrics can be used as feedback tools because

“analytic rubrics provide more detailed, targeted feedback to students about the strengths of their

performance and areas needing attention.” This highlights the importance of recording feedback

in a clear and organized way during rubric-based assessments. In Scormetry 2.0, judges’

comments/notes/follow-up points are stored together with rubric scores in one centralized record

so they can be reviewed after the defense.


2.5 Related Technology


       Universities use many tools for managing student documents, rubric-based scoring, and

evaluation records, but these tools are often separate (documents in one place, scoring in another)

or require manual setup for multi-judge and team-based evaluations.

       document-and-scoring workflows.

3.1 Study Design


        This study uses an applied research approach focused on designing, developing, and

3.3 METHODOLOGICAL ALIGNMENT


3.3.1 Methodology for SOP1: Security Architecture and Access Control


       To address SOP1 regarding controlled access to student documents and evaluation

records, Scormetry 2.0 implements a role- and assignment-based access control model to ensure

that only authorized users can view or manage evaluation materials within each team room.


3.3.1.1 Requirements Modeling


       Object Modeling: The system defines core entities and relationships required for access

control, including Users, Roles, Events, Team Rooms, Judge Assignments, Documents, and

Score Records. This ensures that authorization decisions are consistently tied to event context

and room membership.


       Control Modeling (Role-Based Access Control): Authorization rules are defined by

role and assignment. Students are limited to accessing their own team room for document

upload. Judges can access only team rooms assigned to them for document review, scoring, and

feedback submission. Organizers/Admin have privileges to create events and rooms, assign

judges, and manage rubric states (verification and locking).


3.3.1.2 System Architecture and Security Enforcement


       Network Model and Topology: Scormetry 2.0 follows a web-based client–server

architecture. All requests related to rooms, documents, and evaluation records are processed with

server-side validation to ensure consistent enforcement of access policies.
                                                                                             26


       Security Mechanisms: Security controls include authentication with secure session

handling, authorization checks for protected actions (view room, upload, score, verify, lock,

review records), and controlled-access enforcement to protect confidentiality of student

documents and integrity of evaluation results.


3.3.2 Methodology for SOP 2: Document Access and Retrieval


       To address SOP2 regarding document readiness and fast retrieval, Scormetry 2.0 applies

a centralized document workflow through team rooms, ensuring that judges can access the

correct final documents before and during the defense.


3.3.2.1 Operational Approach


       Students upload only the required final files (final report and presentation slides) into

their team room. The system stores document metadata such as upload timestamp, file type, and

latest version status to prevent wrong-version issues and reduce delays during evaluation

sessions.


3.3.2.2 Requirements Modeling


       Data and process modeling is applied to define the document lifecycle: student upload →

system validation → secure storage with metadata → latest version marking → judge retrieval

from assigned team rooms. This workflow ensures that judges access a single trusted source of

final documents rather than relying on multiple external channels.
                                                                                              27


3.3.2.3 User Interface Design Considerations


        The user interface is designed for efficient retrieval in defense settings. Documents are

clearly separated by type (report vs slides), the latest upload timestamps are displayed, and

judges are provided with direct view/download actions within the assigned team room.


3.3.3 Methodology for SOP 3: Dynamic Rubric Implementation


        To address SOP3 regarding implementing the official fixed dynamic rubric and reducing

scoring workload, Scormetry 2.0 implements a standardized dynamic scoring interface with

automated calculation to minimize formula/weighting errors and reduce manual computation

time.


3.3.3.1 Technical Approach (Dynamic Rubric + Calculation)


        The system converts the official Paragon International University rubric into structured

dynamic rubric components, including criteria and weights, and presents them as a

criterion-by-criterion scoring form.


3.3.3.2 Requirements Modeling


        The scoring workflow is represented using an Input–Process–Output model:


   ●​ Input: Official Paragon International University rubric structure and judge score inputs.


   ●​ Process: Criterion scoring and automated calculation/aggregation logic.


   ●​ Output: Total score/average and final tabulated results.
                                                                                                  28


3.3.3.3 Data Design and Implementation Basis


       Rubric data and scoring records are stored in a structured format to ensure calculation

consistency. The relationship between rubric criteria, weights, score inputs, and score records is

defined to support reliable computation and aggregation across judges.


3.3.4 Methodology for SOP 4: Verification, Validation, and Centralization


       To address SOP4 regarding verified and locked dynamic rubric workflow and centralized

evaluation records, Scormetry 2.0 applies a verification-and-locking workflow and performs

comprehensive testing to ensure the system is reliable for post-defense review.


3.3.4.1 Verification and Locking Workflow


       After dynamic rubric conversion, organizers verify that the converted rubric matches the

official rubric structure. Once verified, the rubric is locked to prevent edits during scoring,

preserving standardization and preventing changes that could affect evaluation integrity.


3.3.4.2 Performance and Risk Considerations


       Performance considerations address simultaneous scoring submissions by multiple judges

during live defense sessions. Risk analysis focuses on issues such as conversion errors caused by

PDF layout quality. These risks are mitigated through organizer verification and rubric locking

before evaluation begins.


3.3.4.3 Validation and Testing Strategy


       Testing is conducted to ensure correctness, usability, and reliability of centralized records.

Unit and integration testing confirm calculation accuracy and end-to-end workflow.
                                                                                               29


Compatibility and performance testing confirm stable operation across common devices and

browsers. Acceptance testing involves Paragon International University faculty using the system

in simulated defense scenarios to validate usability and the ability to review centralized records

after the defense.
